Guarded Stop — the AI-guarded stop order

A price breach only alarms this stop order. An AI guard reads the shape of the move, posts its verdict on-chain, and a Reactive contract sells only on an AI EXECUTE — so a flash wick doesn't dump you at the bottom, but a real trend reversal still gets you out in about a second.

01The problem

A plain threshold stop order sells the instant price touches the stop. In thin pools, price routinely wicks through the stop for a second and bounces back — the mechanical stop sells at the worst price of the day. Telling a wick from a real reversal means reading the shape of the price series around the breach: something a small time-series model does well and a contract cannot do at all. Guarded Stop puts that model inside the execution loop.

Without the guard
Breach = sell. Wick at −3.3% through the stop → sold at the bottom, price recovers 91% within seconds. (Act 1 below is exactly this script.)
With the guard
Breach = pending. The AI observes 30 ticks, posts EXECUTE / VETO / HOLD on-chain, and the Reactive contract acts on the verdict — or on your pre-committed fail-safe if the AI never answers (default: don't sell).

05The AI guard, honestly

The guard is a small gradient-boosting classifier (scikit-learn) that runs locally on CPU — no paid API key, inference in single-digit milliseconds. It is trained on synthetic labelled price series (wick / trend / chop, generated by a seeded script in the repo), so anyone can reproduce the exact model and the exact demo inputs. It proves the loop; it is not trained on real market data, and we say so wherever the numbers appear.

held-out accuracy (test n = 300, stratified)

feature extraction + inference per decision (CPU, after warm-up)

Every verdict is posted on-chain with its confidence score and a reasonHash = keccak256(features + explanation + model version), so any judge can recompute the features off-chain and audit why the guard decided what it decided. If the guard is down or silent, no decision is posted and the contract's timeout fail-safe applies — which defaults to not selling. (That fail-safe fired for real once during our testnet runs, when the guard service wedged: the order timed out and the position was left untouched. See the incident log in DEPLOYMENT.md §4.)
